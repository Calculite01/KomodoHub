Green Sea Turtle

Info

The green sea turtle (Chelonia mydas) is a large, herbivorous marine reptile found in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide. It is the only primarily plant-eating species among sea turtles and plays an essential ecological role in maintaining healthy seagrass beds. The species is classified as endangered due to human exploitation and habitat loss. Green sea turtles have smooth, heart-shaped shells colored brown, olive, or gray with lighter undersides. Their name derives from the green hue of their body fat, caused by a diet of algae and seagrasses. Equipped with powerful flippers, they can migrate thousands of miles between feeding and nesting areas. Adults are mostly herbivorous, while juveniles may also consume jellyfish and small invertebrates.

Threats and that

Green sea turtles are protected under the U.S. Endangered Species Act and listed in CITES Appendix I. Principal threats include entanglement in fishing gear, poaching for meat and eggs, coastal development, pollution, and climate-driven changes to nesting beaches. Global conservation programs—such as marine protected areas and turtle-friendly fishing practices—are helping populations recover, though declines persist in many regions.

Sumatran Tiger

Info

The Sumatran tiger (Panthera tigris sumatrae) is the smallest and rarest living tiger subspecies, found only on the Indonesian island of Sumatra. Critically endangered, it represents the last surviving population of the Sunda Island tigers after the extinction of the Javan tiger and Bali tiger. Its survival is vital to the ecological balance of Sumatra's rainforests. The Sumatran tiger has darker orange fur and more closely spaced black stripes than mainland tigers, a camouflage adaptation to dense jungle environments. Males feature a short mane and longer cheek fur. Webbing between the toes allows strong swimming ability—a trait rarely seen in other tiger subspecies. Each tiger's stripe pattern is unique, functioning much like a fingerprint.

Threats and that

These tigers inhabit about 88,000 km² of fragmented forest across Sumatra, including protected areas such as Kerinci Seblat National Park and Gunung Leuser National Park. Rampant deforestation for palm oil, pulp, and acacia plantations, combined with poaching and human–tiger conflict, has devastated their numbers. Illegal wildlife trade remains a major danger—tiger bones, skins, and teeth are sold despite strict national protection laws.

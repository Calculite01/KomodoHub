Bawean Deer

Info

The Bawean deer (Axis kuhlii), also known as the Kuhl's hog deer or Bawean hog deer, is a small and critically endangered deer species native to Bawean Island, north of Java. It is notable for its extreme rarity, limited range, and ecological importance as the island's only native large herbivore. This deer inhabits the dry and mixed evergreen forests of Bawean Island, typically at low to mid elevations. It is crepuscular, feeding mainly at dawn and dusk on grasses, leaves, and fruits. Social structure is typically limited to small family groups or solitary individuals, reflecting adaptation to the island's constrained habitat and predator-free environment. The Bawean deer is a small-bodied deer, with males typically standing around 65–70 cm at the shoulder. Males possess short, three-tined antlers, while females lack antlers. Its coat is dark brown with a lighter underbelly, and it has relatively short legs adapted for movement through dense forest undergrowth.

Threats

The Bawean deer's limited distribution makes it highly vulnerable to habitat loss and human disturbance. Major threats include deforestation for agriculture, habitat fragmentation, and poaching. Conservation measures led by the Indonesian government and NGOs include habitat protection, anti-poaching patrols, and population monitoring within Bawean Island Nature Reserve. Captive breeding efforts have been minimal due to the species' restricted range.
